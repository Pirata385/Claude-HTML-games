# Ring of Beakers

A bird-fighting and genetics game, drawn in flat vector canvas 2D. You inherit a coop, two chickens and a debt. Net wild birds, breed them, splice their genes, and send them into the ring, where the shape of the body decides the bout.

Starting a new game, choose **Normal** (a coop, two chickens and a debt) or **Sandbox** (every upgrade and tool from day one, no debt, the lab powered and the open sea waiting). In a sandbox game every useful item and every cosmetic is endless (nets, shovels, seed, every feed, gear, hats, beakers and enzymes; relics are not), every fossil is always complete at the lab and can be rebuilt as often as you like for free, and you can sail straight to any island from the sea chart.

Open `index.html` in a browser. It needs no build step and no server, and it runs on desktop and on phones, in portrait or landscape.

## How to play

- **Yard.** Tap a bird for its card. The truck carries as many creatures as you like: whoever rides in it stands in its bed, and you drag birds in and out of it at any time (tap the truck to load or unload everyone). Drag a bird into a nest to breed, onto the dummy to train (each session says which of the bird's numbers go up, and by how much), or into the BFC pick-up box to be rid of it. Pick a feed at the bottom and tap a bird, or the feeder. Sleep ends the day.
- **Map.** WASD or the arrow keys drive (on touch, the left thumb). Click to throw a net; on touch, drag from the net button to aim, or tap it to throw at the nearest animal. The truck button (or `C`) opens a live view of the cage: move the riders about, tap one for its card, or drag one out onto the map to let it go there. `Q` digs, `E` enters a door, `1`-`3` pick a tool, `H` honks. A bird you set free (from the yard or from the truck) leaves its genes in the wild where it was let go: from the next day on, now and then (about one wild animal in forty nearby), one is born carrying some of its genes, and if you catch it you are told whose. The clock button (or `T`) speeds time up x4 or x12, and drops back to normal at nightfall.
- **Time.** Tap the clock in the top bar to wait an hour or until nightfall (19:00), when the Cellar and the Night Market open. Staying out all night costs nothing: at dawn the new day starts wherever you are (the night at home happens as if you had slept there).
- **The map** (the map button) zooms with the buttons, the mouse wheel or a pinch, and drags to look around. Its **Markers** panel switches on pins for kinds of place you want to find: arenas, shops, vets, villages, exotic bird dealers, treasure marks, where each kind of fossil is dug up (the tyrannosaur's volcanic islands, say), where the sea birds live, and islands where an extinct kind still lives. While any are on, an arrow on the road map points to the nearest one, with its name and distance. Out at sea, tap an island you have landed on (they are ringed) and **sail there** in a few hours, or sail home the same way.
- **Money.** Every arena always has two exhibition bouts to watch and bet on; as soon as one is fought the next pair takes its place. Nobody posts odds: the bet window shows both fighters' numbers side by side, and a winning bet pays back three times the stake. If your money ever runs out, Dona Cida pays $50 to scrub the droppings off her henhouse floor (hold and rub each pile).
- **Feeding.** One grain feeds any creature for the day. Birds in the yard walk to the feeder and eat on their own; whatever is left feeds the rest at night. Tap the feeder to choose how many grains to pour in at once (with quick picks for tonight, a week, or all you have); what is not eaten stays for the next night. Food shops and island trading posts also sell a **sack of grain**: 100 grains for $350.
- **Boss prizes.** Beating Old Bira, the Backyard Pit's boss, earns the **silver net**: it holds just like a plain net and never runs out. Beating Dona Zefa at the County Ring earns the **golden shovel**: ten holes a day, every day, before your ordinary shovels are touched.
- **Ring.** Your bird fights on its own. Shout `Sic 'em!`, `Back off!` or `Whistle` (keys `1`-`3`); each has a cooldown, and a bird that does not trust you may ignore you. The flag throws in the towel. Every bout wears a bird out for the day (a tonic restores its vigour so it can fight again); only a loss wounds it, and wounds stay until the vet heals them.
- **Old age.** Nobody dies of old age. A bird that reaches the end of its lifespan **retires**: it stops ageing, and can no longer fight, nest, train or enter the sideshow. Drive it to **Sunset Roost**, where Dona Nina looks after retired birds for free: they live in her garden, potter about, peck, and come running when she scatters grain. Tap a resident for its card, or take it home again. BFC still takes retired birds too, and an elixir (or a whole-genome clone with a longer life) can bring one back to the ring.
- **Night.** After dark the day birds roost and the night birds come out in their place, in about the same numbers: owls, kiwis, nightjars, potoos, kakapos and thick-knees, night herons, penguins coming ashore.
- **The open sea.** Win the Grand Ring and pay off the bank, and Biu's Garage will fit a **speedboat kit**: drive into water and the truck becomes a speedboat. Past the mainland's shores the sea goes on for ever, dotted with islands made from the save's seed: mostly open water, with an island in about one stretch of sea in three, and, just past the mainland's own waters, a ring of eight big islands, one of each land (farmland, wetland, savanna, highlands, jungle, tundra, volcanic rock and desert). Out on open water, flocks of sea birds (or a lone bird) turn up now and then: passing over on migration in a ragged V, wheeling over a shoal and dropping onto the water to feed, or riding the swell in a raft. Down on the water they can be netted, though most take off when the boat gets close. Islands of your own from older saves stay where they were, even where the chart has changed around them. Each has its own land (among them tundra, volcanic rock and desert, found nowhere else), its own birds (a local race of each kind: giants, dwarfs, other colours, crests; a few strangers from far away; now and then a kind thought extinct), albatrosses, frigatebirds and puffins live nowhere else, the islands' ground holds fossils of their own (Haast's eagle, the great auk, Pelagornis), and the bigger ones have villages, a trading post and a bird doctor, or an arena with a boss of its own. The further out, the wilder the islands and the harder their arenas. The map button turns into a sea chart of the waters around you.
- **Breeding islands.** Out at sea you can have an island of your own. Land on one nobody lives on and **claim** it ($500), or stop the boat in open water, away from the mainland and other islands, and **raise** a new one from the sea ($2500), choosing what it is made of. Each gets a field station. Leave birds there from the truck (and people you netted: they live there just the same, and pair up with whoever is around) and they live on their own: every night the pairs may raise young, up to what the island can feed (14 on the smallest, 46 on the biggest), the old pass away in time, and generation follows generation, all mixing with one another. Take any of them back whenever you like. Leave **odd mushrooms** or **isotopes** at the station and the island turns radioactive: young born while it is hot come out mutated and deformed (odd genes, extreme bodies, now and then teeth, two heads, a glow or extra legs), the hotter the stranger. The radiation fades a little each night, or a $300 clean-up clears it. Your islands show on the sea chart and have a marker of their own. The station's **Stay** tab camps there for a night, three, a week, two weeks or a month in one go, to watch generations go by: every night counts as a night at home, and your birds at home and in the truck are fed from the grain in your bag (the tab says how many nights it lasts).
- **Lab.** Three fuses power it. Every gene is mapped from the start: pick any creature in the truck to see its fourteen chromosome pairs and what each gene does. Picking a gene makes the other genes of the same trait glow green (pick Muscle I and Muscle II and III light up). **Make female** / **Make male** changes a bird's or a person's sex for good (a male's second Z becomes a W; a female's Z is doubled). Extract one copy of a gene into a beaker, or copy the **whole genome** into one beaker. Edit any base at the CRISPR bench (a whole-genome beaker opens on a chromosome grid, so you can edit any of its genes). Each cut is a small mini-game; switch it off in Settings and edits apply at once, as many as you like. Then inject the beaker into another creature or an egg. A whole genome turns the receiver, bird or person, into a **clone** of the donor at the receiver's own age, and its beaker is kept, so a favourite bird can be kept for ever. Beakers you no longer want can be thrown away. Everything in the lab is free. Before an injection, a preview shows the creature as it is and as it would be (an egg as the adult it grows into), with its stats and what changes; nothing happens until you confirm. Giving or receiving genes leaves a creature worn out for the day, never hurt.

The Codex (in the yard) has a longer guide, the kinds of bird you have met, the gene atlas and the feats.

## What is in it

- **Genetics.** Every creature has 14 chromosome pairs of A/C/G/T bases, 140 genes at fixed places, two copies of each. About 80 traits are read from them: size, neck, beak, legs, spurs, wings, tail, comb, colours and patterns, muscle, lungs, nerve, lifespan, voice, and rare switches such as teeth, arms, a second head or a glow. Birds are ZW: males carry two Z chromosomes and females one, so some traits follow the sex. Eggs come from meiosis with crossing-over and a little mutation.
- **Colours.** Colour genes are read base by base, so any difference in them shows: no two birds are quite alike, domestic kinds come in many shades, and black (dominant) and white (recessive) morphs turn up now and then.
- **61 kinds**, from quail to ostrich, among them the Andean condor (in the highlands), the harpy eagle (in the jungle), the shoebill (in the wetlands, clattering its huge bill), the crowned crane (savanna and wetlands), the laughing kookaburra and the marabou stork; seven night birds (the nightjar, the potoo, the kakapo, the thick-knee, the frogmouth, the oilbird of the jungle and highlands, and the boat-billed heron of the wetlands); the cormorant on the coast; five birds that live only on the islands of the open sea (the albatross, the frigatebird, the puffin, and on warm islands the red-footed booby and the tropicbird); and thirteen extinct kinds only reachable through fossils (or on a remote island): the titanic **elephant bird**, the heaviest bird there is; **Gastornis**, a giant flightless bird dug up under farmland; four that are dug up only out at sea (**Haast's eagle**, the **great auk**, **Pelagornis**, a giant seabird with bony teeth, and **Pteranodon**, a flying reptile); and two dinosaurs, the **tyrannosaurus** (Bone Quarry and volcanic islands) and the ostrich-like **Gallimimus** (Bone Quarry only). A kind is only a region of the gene space, so anything can have young with anything: a hummingbird with an ostrich, or a hen with a person. Each parent's copy of a gene carries its own dominance, so a cross can take whole body parts after one parent, blend others, and get its colours from either side. Crosses are named after both parents and breed true to their new kind.
- **Physics fights.** Fighters are ragdolls built from their genes. They peck, kick with their spurs, buffet with their wings, shove and punch; damage comes from the speed of the blow where it lands. Nobody falls out of a ring: every bout is fought in a walled pit, and each arena has its own twist instead. At the Backyard Pit the crowd tosses grain in (a fighter that eats it gets its wind back); the County Ring has a bog in the middle that slows you down and takes your feet; at the City Arena big fans blow gusts that push light birds about; in the Cellar the lights go out now and then and only big eyes and a steady head find their mark; the Grand Ring fights three rounds with a breather between, and rounds won count on points. Island arenas pick one of these.
- **A seeded map** with six biomes, each with its own animals by day and by night. The day's wild population lives all over the map and stays put while you go in and out of places; it is renewed each morning, and about one wild animal in 170 is a natural cross of two kinds. The map also has roads, nineteen sites and twelve houses. Animals graze, bolt, fly off or charge the truck depending on their genes. Digging almost always turns something up. At the **Bone Quarry**, east of the Sideshow, Dr. Ossa has roped off a pit where most finds are fossil fragments, about a third of them from the elephant bird and now and then a tyrannosaur; her tent keeps count of every set you have and says where each is found. Holes you dig trap small animals and fill back in overnight, when the ground holds something new; a red X is a one-time find whose hole stays.
- **People.** Every neighbour buys birds (paying more for what they wish for), sells a few of their own, and takes yours in trade for credit; breeders keep more stock, collectors also buy relics and trainers also challenge you. Madame Zuleica's **Curio Aviary** in town sells the strangest birds there are: crosses of kinds that have no business together, with teeth, two heads, a glow, arms or extra legs. They can be netted too, which the sheriff minds: each person netted makes you wanted, and the heat dies down in one night per person netted since it was last at zero (one person, one night; two, two; three or more, three). Shopkeepers, arena bosses, the vet, the banker and the professor never come outside, but you can net them indoors: tap the portrait in the corner of their place. A netted person can share a nest with a bird, live on one of your islands, or go to BFC. A place whose person is away stays shut (and one sold to BFC stands empty for three days until someone new moves in), unless you **put someone in charge**: any person you netted, or any bird in the truck. A bird put in charge wakes up **sentient** and carries on the trade under its own name, squawking now and then. Net the stand-in to take it back as it was; let the real owner go and they take their place back, and the stand-in hops into your truck. Net every one of the thirty people on the mainland (the feat **Ghost town**; the Codex counts them).
- **BFC, Brazil's Fried Chicken.** Takes any bird (or person) you are done with, by the kilo, at the counter or from the box in your yard. A parody: the look is a fried chicken place, the mascot (Coronel Galo, a rooster in a white suit) and everything else are this game's own.
- Five arenas, all open from the start except the Grand Ring, which only takes the champion of the other four; each has one bout a day in every weight class from Gnat to Titan (so any bird you own has a fight), bosses and betting, a sideshow with three contests, an ending, twenty-three feats, synthesised sound, eleven tunes (each place has its own, with a night tune on the map and a heavier one for boss bouts), bird calls by family (clucks, crows, quacks, honks, gobbles, hoots, screeches, caws, trills, booms, brays, a harpy's wail, a condor's hiss; a cross has the calls of both its kinds, and no two calls are quite alike), three save slots with export and import.

## Files

```
index.html          the page: a canvas and the scripts, in dependency order
js/core.js          seeded random numbers and maths
js/genome.js        chromosomes, genes, meiosis, mutation
js/species.js       the kinds of creature, and telling which kind one is
js/phenotype.js     genes -> body, colours and fighting numbers
js/pose.js          standing, walking, flying and sitting poses
js/physics.js       the particle world
js/creature.js      the ragdoll: gait, balance, attacks
js/fight.js         a bout: brains, damage, commands, odds
js/items.js  world.js  npc.js  economy.js  lab.js  state.js   the rules
js/sea.js           the open sea: endless islands, their birds, villages and arenas; raising your own
js/birdart.js  props.js    drawing creatures and scenery
js/input.js  ui.js  audio.js  save.js  common.js  main.js     the shell
js/scenes/          title, coop, ring, map, sites, lab, codex, chores, roost
tests/              logic tests
android/            the Android app (APK) wrapper: see android/README.md
```

Everything up to `state.js` runs without a browser, which is how the tests and the betting odds work.

## Notes

- Settings (the gear button) can switch off the gene-edit mini-game and the goal hints at the top of the screen, for a cleaner view.
- Add `?scene=coop&seed=7` to the address to jump into a throwaway game that is never saved (also `map`, `lab`, `codex`).
- Saves live in the browser's local storage. The bag button on the title screen exports a save as text and imports one.
- `android/build.sh` builds an Android APK of the game, locked to landscape (see `android/README.md`).
- The game is a spiritual successor to the breed-and-race genre, with its own theme, world, art and text.
