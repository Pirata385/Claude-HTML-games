# Ring of Beakers

A bird-fighting and genetics game, drawn in flat vector canvas 2D. You inherit a coop, two chickens and a debt. Net wild birds, breed them, splice their genes, and send them into the ring, where the shape of the body decides the bout.

Starting a new game, choose **Normal** (a coop, two chickens and a debt) or **Sandbox** (every upgrade and tool from day one, no debt, the lab powered and the open sea waiting). In a sandbox game every useful item and every cosmetic is endless (nets, shovels, seed, every feed, gear, hats, beakers and enzymes; relics are not), every fossil is always complete at the lab and can be rebuilt as often as you like for free, and you can sail straight to any island from the sea chart.

Open `index.html` in a browser. It needs no build step and no server, and it runs on desktop and on phones, in portrait or landscape.

## How to play

- **Yard.** Tap a bird for its card. The card has three tabs: **Card** (the bird and its numbers), **Family** (its mother and father, drawn if you still have them or the Hall of Fame remembers them, where they are now, a button to open their cards, and the young it has had that are still with you; a starter, a wild-caught or a bought bird has no parents on record) and, in a sandbox game, **Edit**, where you set any number by hand (drag along a bar or use − and +, up to the weight class's cap) and the change is real: power scales the bird's weapons, toughness its health, stamina its wind, speed its running, and guts, wits and fury its nerve, head and temper, so it shows in bouts; **Reset to its genes** gives back what its genes and training give. The truck carries as many creatures as you like: whoever rides in it stands in its bed, and you drag birds in and out of it at any time (tap the truck to load or unload everyone). Drag a bird into a nest to breed, onto the dummy to train (each session says which of the bird's numbers go up, and by how much: one session a day, each adding 3% to one kind of training, up to 35%; in training weights a session adds 4.5%; a tonic gives two more sessions that day, once a day; a sandbox bird on eternal vigor trains and gains like any other), or into the BFC pick-up box to be rid of it. Pick a feed at the bottom and tap a bird, or the feeder. Sleep ends the day.
- **Map.** WASD or the arrow keys drive (on touch, the left thumb). Zoom out to see up to twice as far (and back in to the usual view) with the mouse wheel, the - and = keys, or the − + buttons above the clock; the zoom is kept for next time. Click to throw a net; on touch, drag from the net button to aim, or tap it to throw at the nearest animal. The truck button (or `C`) opens a live view of the cage: move the riders about, tap one for its card, or drag one out onto the map to let it go there. A rider's card lets you use the bag on it wherever you are, on the road or out at sea: **Give** hands it feed, worms, chili, a tonic, Gro-Mash, love seeds, an odd mushroom, an isotope or the youth elixir, and **Wear** dresses it in hats, accessories and fighting kit, just as in the yard. `Q` digs, `E` enters a door, `1`-`3` pick a tool, `H` honks. A bird you set free (from the yard or from the truck) lives on near where it was let go, under its own name (shown above it when you drive close) and still wearing its hat; net it again and it is the same bird, its record, training and all, a few days older. Out there it keeps growing older day by day: the night after its last day (its lifespan) it dies of old age in the wild, the morning report says so, and it is gone from the map (its genes stay in the wild around there for a while). It also leaves its genes in the wild there: from the next day on, now and then (about one wild animal in forty nearby), one is born carrying some of its genes, and if you catch it you are told whose. The clock button (or `T`) speeds time up x4 or x12, and drops back to normal at nightfall.
- **Time.** Tap the clock in the top bar to wait an hour or until nightfall (19:00), when the Cellar and the Night Market open. Staying out all night costs nothing: at dawn the new day starts wherever you are (the night at home happens as if you had slept there).
- **The map** (the map button) zooms with the buttons, the mouse wheel or a pinch, and drags to look around. Its **Markers** panel switches on pins for kinds of place you want to find: arenas, shops, vets, villages, exotic bird dealers, treasure marks, where each kind of fossil is dug up (the tyrannosaur's volcanic islands, say), where the sea birds live, and islands where an extinct kind still lives. While any are on, an arrow on the road map points to the nearest one, with its name and distance. Out at sea, tap an island you have landed on (they are ringed) and **sail there** in a few hours, or sail home the same way: the **Home** button jumps the chart back to the mainland and picks it, so however far out you are, home is one tap and **Sail there** away (the **Truck** button jumps back to where you are).
- **Money.** Every arena always has two exhibition bouts to watch and bet on; as soon as one is fought the next pair takes its place. The bet window shows both fighters' numbers side by side and what each side pays: the house guesses each fighter's chance from those numbers (and how hurt they are) and pays a little over the odds, so the favourite pays little (from 1.2 times the stake), an even bout about 2.4 times and the underdog up to 4 times. A good eye still comes out ahead over time, but no longer by much; prize money for winning a bout is unchanged. If your money ever runs out, or a lost bet leaves you less than the cheapest entry fee ($15), Dona Cida pays $50 to scrub the droppings off her henhouse floor (hold and rub each pile).
- **Feeding.** One grain feeds any creature for the day. Birds in the yard walk to the feeder and eat on their own; whatever is left feeds the rest at night. Tap the feeder to choose how many grains to pour in at once (with quick picks for tonight, a week, or all you have); what is not eaten stays for the next night. Food shops and island trading posts also sell a **sack of grain**: 100 grains for $350.
- **Holding a title.** An arena you have won is held by the bird that won it. If that bird retires, or leaves you (sold, sent to BFC, set free, traded, or dead of old age on an island), the title stands empty and a day later a new champion claims it, with a bird of their own (and a slightly bigger purse). Your wins there still count: go back and beat them to take the title again. Having been champion of all four other arenas keeps the Grand Ring open, title or not.
- **Boss prizes.** Beating Old Bira, the Backyard Pit's boss, earns the **silver net**: it holds just like a plain net and never runs out. Beating Dona Zefa at the County Ring earns the **golden shovel**: ten holes a day, every day, before your ordinary shovels are touched.
- **Ring.** Your bird fights on its own. Shout `Sic 'em!`, `Back off!` or `Whistle` (keys `1`-`3`); each has a cooldown, and a bird that does not trust you may ignore you. When a bird goes down, the other steps back and gives it room to get up; only `Sic 'em!` sends it in on a bird that is down. The flag throws in the towel. Every bout wears a bird out for the day (a tonic restores its vigour so it can fight again); only a loss wounds it, and wounds stay until the vet heals them.
- **Habits.** About half the birds settle into a quirk of their own once they are grown, shown on their card: some walk in circles, some chase the others about, some stare at the feeder or the BFC box for ages, some sing at odd hours (even in the middle of the night), and some have it in for one particular bird and keep pecking at it. The rest just get on with being birds.
- **Happenings in the yard.** Now and then (not often, and purely for show) two birds square off over a patch of the yard, puffing up and pecking at the air until they lose interest; a wild bird wanders in from the fields, the flock stares and the bold ones see it off; or a bird finds something strange in the grass (a rubber duck, an odd sock, a mysterious egg-shaped stone) and makes up its mind about it.
- **Hall of Fame.** The board by the gate keeps the birds that made your story, but only birds you put to use: ones you fought with, entered in the sideshow, or caught with numbers few of their kind ever have (about one in twenty). Birds born on your islands stay out while they live there; take one home from the field station and it counts like any other bird (fight it, show it, or bring home one with outstanding numbers). Its tabs sort them into arena champions; fighters (veterans of ten wins, legends of twenty-five); records (most wins, most sideshow wins, heaviest, oldest, fastest, counted over every bird you ever had); sideshow stars (three wins, or ten for a legend); talents (caught in the wild, or taken off your island, with outstanding numbers); retired standouts (birds that made a name, with a title, a record or three wins, and then grew old in your care: retired champions, fighters and sideshow stars); and curious stories (two heads, a walking mutation, back from extinction, a clone, a change of sex, back from the wild, a bird that ran a business). Each has its stats, titles and a short biography written from what happened to it, and stays on the board after it is gone, with how it left.
- **Wardrobe.** Every bird (and person) can wear one thing in each place: a hat (top hats to Viking helmets, sombreros, wizard hats, traffic cones), something on the face (sunglasses, monocles, heart glasses, a fake moustache, a clown nose), round the neck (bow ties, neckties, pearls, a cowbell, a ruff), on the back (a hero cape, a tiny backpack or saddle, a toy jetpack, a turtle shell), on the feet (sneakers, rain boots, roller skates, bunny slippers) and on the tail (a ribbon, a flag, a sparkler, a pom-pom, streamers). Madame Odete sells most of them; eleven are prizes for feats (a halo, a chef hat, a champion's medal, angel wings, a burglar mask, lab goggles...). One piece of fighting kit on top: besides spurs, a helmet, a scarf and training weights, a steel beak tip, a chain-mail vest, wing wax, a lucky horseshoe, spring boots and a pocket tonic each give a small edge.
- **Old age.** Nobody dies of old age. A bird that reaches the end of its lifespan **retires**: it stops ageing, and can no longer fight, nest, train or enter the sideshow. Drive it to **Sunset Roost**, where Dona Nina looks after retired birds for free. From outside it is the same cottage as ever; inside is a big fenced yard seen from above, like the world outside, that grows with the number of residents (drag to look around when it does not fit). The birds wander the grass and peck about, and come running when she scatters grain; at nightfall they settle on the straw under the lean-to and sleep, and she goes indoors (her window lights up) until morning. Time passes in there as it does on the map. New residents walk in through the gate. Tap a resident for its card, or take it home again; the **Residents** button lists them all. BFC still takes retired birds too, and an elixir (or a whole-genome clone with a longer life) can bring one back to the ring. (Out on your islands, and in the wild after you set them free, birds do die of old age.)
- **Tonic of eternal vigor.** The Night Market's dearest bottle ($9000; endless in a sandbox game, like any feed), given from the feed bar or, out on the road, from a rider's card. In a **normal** game the bird never dies (not on your islands, not set free in the wild; only BFC can take it) and can nest and lay for ever, even once retired, but it still retires on time and a retired bird still cannot fight, train or enter the sideshow. In a **sandbox** game it has no limits at all: the bird never retires, its body stays in its prime however many days it counts (a bird already old comes back to its best), and it fights, trains, shows and breeds for ever. A bird takes it once; its card shows it.
- **Night.** After dark the day birds roost and the night birds come out in their place, in about the same numbers: owls, kiwis, nightjars, potoos, kakapos and thick-knees, night herons, penguins coming ashore.
- **The open sea.** Win the Grand Ring and pay off the bank, and Biu's Garage will fit a **speedboat kit**: drive into water and the truck becomes a speedboat. Past the mainland's shores the sea goes on for ever, dotted with islands made from the save's seed: mostly open water, with an island in about one stretch of sea in three, and, just past the mainland's own waters, a ring of eight big islands, one of each land (farmland, wetland, savanna, highlands, jungle, tundra, volcanic rock and desert). Out on open water, flocks of sea birds (or a lone bird) turn up now and then: passing over on migration in a ragged V, wheeling over a shoal and dropping onto the water to feed, or riding the swell in a raft. Down on the water they can be netted, though most take off when the boat gets close. Islands of your own from older saves stay where they were, even where the chart has changed around them. Each has its own land (among them tundra, volcanic rock and desert, found nowhere else), its own birds (a local race of each kind: giants, dwarfs, other colours, crests; a few strangers from far away; now and then a kind thought extinct), albatrosses, frigatebirds and puffins live nowhere else, the islands' ground holds fossils of their own (Haast's eagle, the great auk, Pelagornis), and the bigger ones have villages, a trading post and a bird doctor, or an arena with a boss of its own. The further out, the wilder the islands and the harder their arenas. The map button turns into a sea chart of the waters around you.
- **Breeding islands.** Out at sea you can have an island of your own. Land on one nobody lives on and **claim** it ($500), or stop the boat in open water, away from the mainland and other islands, and **raise** a new one from the sea ($2500), choosing what it is made of. Each gets a field station. Leave birds there from the truck (and people you netted: they live there just the same, and pair up with whoever is around) and they live on their own: every night the pairs may raise young, up to what the island can feed (14 on the smallest, 46 on the biggest), the old pass away in time, and generation follows generation, all mixing with one another. Take any of them back whenever you like: the station's list sorts, highest or lowest first, by generation, power, toughness, guts, fury, speed, stamina, wits, all the numbers together, age, weight or size (and remembers the choice). Leave **odd mushrooms** or **isotopes** at the station and the island turns radioactive: young born while it is hot come out mutated and deformed (odd genes, extreme bodies, now and then teeth, two heads, a glow or extra legs), the hotter the stranger. The radiation fades a little each night, or a $300 clean-up clears it. Your islands show on the sea chart and have a marker of their own. The station's **Stay** tab camps there for a night, three, a week, two weeks or a month in one go, to watch generations go by: every night counts as a night at home, and your birds at home and in the truck are fed from the grain in your bag (the tab says how many nights it lasts).
- **Lab.** Three fuses power it. Every gene is mapped from the start: pick any creature in the truck to see its fourteen chromosome pairs and what each gene does. Picking a gene makes the other genes of the same trait glow green (pick Muscle I and Muscle II and III light up). **Make female** / **Make male** changes a bird's or a person's sex for good (a male's second Z becomes a W; a female's Z is doubled). Extract one copy of a gene into a beaker, or copy the **whole genome** into one beaker. Edit any base at the CRISPR bench (a whole-genome beaker opens on a chromosome grid, so you can edit any of its genes). Each cut is a small mini-game; switch it off in Settings and edits apply at once, as many as you like. Then inject the beaker into another creature or an egg. A single-gene beaker remembers the copy it was taken from (A or B) and by default goes back into that same copy of the receiver's gene, leaving the other copy as it was; the injector can also put it into the other copy or into both (a female has a single copy of each Z gene, which either choice writes). A whole genome turns the receiver, bird or person, into a **clone** of the donor at the receiver's own age, and its beaker is kept, so a favourite bird can be kept for ever. Beakers you no longer want can be thrown away. In a normal game the lab charges for three things: reading a creature's genome (once, $40: until then its genes cannot be seen, taken out or copied), each base changed at the CRISPR bench ($15) and each injection ($50, or $250 for a clone). Taking genes out, copying a whole genome, throwing beakers away and changing sex stay free, and a sandbox game pays for nothing. (In an older save, the creatures it already had count as read.) Before an injection, a preview shows the creature as it is and as it would be (an egg as the adult it grows into), with its stats and what changes; nothing happens until you confirm. Giving or receiving genes leaves a creature worn out for the day, never hurt. The **Guide** tab explains how genes work (two copies, four bases, dominance, genes I/II/III, switches, the Z chromosome) and then, group by group, what every kind of gene does in the game: what it changes in a fight, in the sideshow, in breeding or in looks, how it is inherited and which of its genes sit on Z. The **?** button on a gene in the Genome tab jumps to its entry.

The Codex (in the yard) has a longer guide, the kinds of bird you have met, the gene atlas and the feats.

## What is in it

- **Genetics.** Every creature has 14 chromosome pairs of A/C/G/T bases, 140 genes at fixed places, two copies of each. About 80 traits are read from them: size, neck, beak, legs, spurs, wings, tail, comb, colours and patterns, muscle, lungs, nerve, lifespan, voice, and rare switches such as teeth, arms, a second head or a glow. Birds are ZW: males carry two Z chromosomes and females one, so some traits follow the sex. Eggs come from meiosis with crossing-over and a little mutation.
- **Colours.** Colour genes are read base by base, so any difference in them shows: no two birds are quite alike, domestic kinds come in many shades, and black (dominant) and white (recessive) morphs turn up now and then.
- **72 kinds**, from quail to ostrich, among them the Andean condor (in the highlands), the harpy eagle (in the jungle), the shoebill (in the wetlands, clattering its huge bill), the crowned crane (savanna and wetlands), the laughing kookaburra and the marabou stork; birds with bills that could not be built before (the **roseate spoonbill** and the **scarlet ibis** of the wetlands, the **pied avocet** and the **black skimmer** of the coast, the **red crossbill** of the highlands and tundra, the **sword-billed hummingbird** of the highlands, the **great hornbill** of the jungle and savanna); two **birds-of-paradise** in the jungle (the greater, with its fountain of golden plumes, and the tiny crimson king); seven night birds (the nightjar, the potoo, the kakapo, the thick-knee, the frogmouth, the oilbird of the jungle and highlands, and the boat-billed heron of the wetlands); the cormorant on the coast; five birds that live only on the islands of the open sea (the albatross, the frigatebird, the puffin, and on warm islands the red-footed booby and the tropicbird); and fourteen extinct kinds only reachable through fossils (or on a remote island): the titanic **elephant bird**, the heaviest bird there is; **Gastornis**, a giant flightless bird dug up under farmland; four that are dug up only out at sea (**Haast's eagle**, the **great auk**, **Pelagornis**, a giant seabird with bony teeth, and **Pteranodon**, a flying reptile); and three dinosaurs, the **tyrannosaurus** with its toothy jaws (Bone Quarry and volcanic islands), the ostrich-like **Gallimimus** (Bone Quarry only) and the **Spinosaurus**, with a crocodile's long snout and a great sail on its back (Bone Quarry and desert islands). A kind is only a region of the gene space, so anything can have young with anything: a hummingbird with an ostrich, or a hen with a person. Each parent's copy of a gene carries its own dominance, so a cross can take whole body parts after one parent, blend others, and get its colours from either side. Crosses are named after both parents and breed true to their new kind; however many kinds mix over the generations, a cross is named after the two that make up most of it, so names stay short.
- **Bill shapes.** A gene that used to be junk DNA, **Bill shape**, is read letter by letter: a copy ending in TA spells a shape with its first two letters (a spoon, a sickle curved down, an awl curved up, crossed tips, a helmet on top, a lower half longer than the upper, a needle longer than the bird, a tyrannosaur's toothy jaws, a spinosaur's long toothy snout), and the bill takes it when both copies spell the same one. Shapes change the peck (jaws bite 45% harder, a spoon 25% weaker). Another former junk locus, **Back sail**, grows a spinosaur's sail when both copies read TATA. Junk almost never spells either by chance, so birds from before keep their bills; edit both copies at the CRISPR bench to give any bird a new one (the lab's Guide lists every code). Tyrannosaurs you already had grow their jaws when the save loads.
- **Physics fights.** Fighters are ragdolls built from their genes. They peck, kick with their spurs, buffet with their wings, shove and punch; damage comes from the speed of the blow where it lands. Long-legged waders (herons, flamingos, cranes, storks, spoonbills, ibises, avocets, the secretary bird...) are a body type of their own: they kick with a little hop rather than a great leap and close in to peck instead of leaping in from afar, and when the foe falls back they fall back a step too, then hurl themselves at it in a **flying kick**, a long low leap feet first that lands much harder than an ordinary kick ("FLYING KICK!"). When one lands, **impact frames** flash: the bout freezes for a moment on two stark frames, black with white cut-outs and then white with black, speed lines bursting from the blow. A flying kick also knocks the foe flat and sends it flying, limp, into the wall behind it ("SLAM!"), and the whole place shakes; the damage is the kick's own. Nobody falls out of a ring: every bout is fought in a walled pit, and each arena has its own twist instead. At the Backyard Pit the crowd tosses grain in (a fighter that eats it gets its wind back); the County Ring has a bog in the middle that slows you down and takes your feet; at the City Arena big fans blow gusts that push light birds about; in the Cellar the lights go out now and then and only big eyes and a steady head find their mark; the Grand Ring fights best of three rounds of thirty seconds with a breather between: a knockout (or a bird that runs) takes the round, time decides it on points, and the beaten bird gets back up for the next. Island arenas pick one of these. Every blow is scaled to the weight class, the same for both birds, so a bout lasts about twenty-five seconds on average whatever the size of the fighters (small birds used to tap at each other until time ran out, big ones were done in fifteen seconds). Eight neighbours come to watch each bout, four outside each wall of the pit (people from your own world: whoever is at home and not in your truck, now and then Coronel Galo himself). Those on the left cheer for the bird on the left and those on the right for the other: arms up and jumping when their bird lands a big one, a fist pumped for a small one, hands on the head when it takes one or goes down, a startled step back from a bird slammed into their wall, clapping between rounds. The stands behind the boards are full too.
- **A seeded map** with six biomes, each with its own animals by day and by night. The day's wild population lives all over the map and stays put while you go in and out of places; it is renewed each morning, and about one wild animal in 170 is a natural cross of two kinds. The map also has roads, nineteen sites and twelve houses. Animals graze, bolt, fly off or charge the truck depending on their genes. Digging almost always turns something up. At the **Bone Quarry**, east of the Sideshow, Dr. Ossa has roped off a pit where most finds are fossil fragments, about a third of them from the elephant bird and now and then a tyrannosaur; her tent keeps count of every set you have and says where each is found. Holes you dig trap small animals and fill back in overnight, when the ground holds something new; a red X is a one-time find whose hole stays.
- **People.** Every neighbour buys birds (paying more for what they wish for), sells a few of their own, and takes yours in trade for credit; breeders keep more stock, collectors also buy relics and trainers also challenge you. Madame Zuleica's **Curio Aviary** in town sells the strangest birds there are: crosses of kinds that have no business together, with teeth, two heads, a glow, arms or extra legs. They can be netted too, which the sheriff minds: each person netted makes you wanted, and the heat dies down in one night per person netted since it was last at zero (one person, one night; two, two; three or more, three). Shopkeepers, arena bosses, the vet, the banker and the professor never come outside, but you can net them indoors: tap the portrait in the corner of their place. A netted person can share a nest with a bird, live on one of your islands, or go to BFC. A place whose person is away stays shut (and one sold to BFC stands empty for three days until someone new moves in), unless you **put someone in charge**: any person you netted, or any bird in the truck. A bird put in charge wakes up **sentient** and carries on the trade under its own name, squawking now and then. Net the stand-in to take it back as it was; let the real owner go and they take their place back, and the stand-in hops into your truck. Net every one of the thirty-one people on the mainland, the Coronel included (the feat **Ghost town**; the Codex counts them).
- **BFC, Brazil's Fried Chicken.** Takes any bird (or person) you are done with, by the kilo, at the counter or from the box in your yard. Its **Island contract** tab signs you up for the van to call at your islands every night: it takes every bird there that is at least the age you set (people stay put), from the islands you put on the list, pays what the pick-up box pays, and by default leaves the last grown male and female of each island so the colony goes on; the morning report says what went. A parody: the look is a fried chicken place, the mascot and everything else are this game's own. It is run by **Coronel Galo** himself, a rooster-man: a rooster's head with comb and wattles, white feathers, a man's height and arms, a white suit with a string tie, a white planter's hat and a cane. Like every shopkeeper he can be netted indoors (tap his portrait at the counter): he rides in the truck suit, hat, cane and all, BFC stays shut (and its van skips your pick-up box) until you let him go or put someone else in charge, and if he is gone for good (sold to BFC, say), his son, Coronel Galo Jr., takes the counter in the same white suit a few days later.
- Five arenas, all open from the start except the Grand Ring, which only takes someone who has been champion of the other four; each has one bout a day in every weight class from Gnat to Titan (so any bird you own has a fight; the classes also cap the physical numbers on a bird's card, power, toughness and stamina, at 75 for a Gnat, then 115, 150, 190, 225 and 265, up to 300 for a Titan, while speed and the mind's numbers stay within 100, and every bar fills at its own class's cap; the numbers are read against the class, so an ordinary wild bird shows about 45% of its class's cap and the best bred and trained ones reach it, while prices, the betting odds and what people wish for still read them on the old even scale), bosses and betting, a sideshow with three contests, an ending, twenty-four feats (eleven with a wearable prize; the newest, **Bird town**, is for putting a bird in charge of every house and business on the mainland, in place of the people), synthesised sound effects, a recorded soundtrack of fourteen tracks (a main theme, two day and two night themes, the open sea by day and by night, the arenas' lobby, three fight themes, the lab and the night shops; out in the open the tracks follow one another, or, with **Music plays: now and then** in Settings, come and go with stretches of quiet in between, while the menu, places with music of their own and fights always play; it plays as game sound through the Web Audio API, mixed with whatever else is playing, so it never stops the music app you were listening to or shows media controls, except when the game is opened straight from disk, where it falls back to plain audio elements), bird calls by family (clucks, crows, quacks, honks, gobbles, hoots, screeches, caws, trills, booms, brays, a harpy's wail, a condor's hiss; a cross has the calls of both its kinds, and no two calls are quite alike), three save slots with export and import.

## Notes

- Settings (the gear button) can switch off the gene-edit mini-game, the goal hints at the top of the screen (for a cleaner view) and the impact frames of a flying kick.
- Add `?scene=coop&seed=7` to the address to jump into a throwaway game that is never saved (also `map`, `lab`, `codex`).
- Saves live in the browser's local storage. The bag button on the title screen exports a save as text and imports one. If the browser ever refuses to store a save (its storage full), the game says so instead of carrying on as if it had saved. (Older builds could grow a save past that limit after many generations of crosses on your islands, and then silently stopped saving; loading such a save now shrinks it back.)
- `android/build.sh` builds an Android APK of the game, locked to landscape (see `android/README.md`).
- The game is a spiritual successor to the breed-and-race genre, with its own theme, world, art and text.
